\subsection{The band we want}
Among all bands that cover every class in every horizon bin, which is the shortest?
Forecasts are heteroscedastic, so we measure length in units of the forecast's own scale through a weight $\omega(x)>0$ (below, $\omega=1/\bar\sigma$).
Relax $C$ to $I(x,y)\in[0,1]$, the probability that $y$ is included.

\begin{proposition}[oracle band]
\label{prop:oracle}
Fix a horizon cell $b$ with $\pi_{c,b}>0$ for all $c$, and assume $Y$ has a Lebesgue density given $X,Z$.
Assume the problem $\min_C \E_{Q_b}\big[\omega(X)\,|C(X)|\big]$ subject to $Q_b(Y\in C(X)\mid Z=c)\ge1-\alpha$ for all $c$ has a feasible band of finite weighted length that covers every class strictly above $1-\alpha$.
Then the problem has a solution of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:oracle}
C^\star(x)=\Big\{y:\ \tfrac{1}{\omega(x)}\textstyle\sum_{c=1}^K v^\star_{c}\,p_c(x)\,f_c(y\mid x)\ \ge 1\Big\}
\end{equation}
up to randomisation on its boundary $\{\sum_c v^\star_cp_cf_c=\omega\}$.
Here $v^\star_c=\lambda^\star_c/\pi_{c,b}$, and $\lambda^\star\ge0$ maximises the concave dual $(1-\alpha)\sum_c\lambda_c-\E_{Q_b}\!\int\big(\sum_c\tfrac{\lambda_c}{\pi_{c,b}}p_cf_c-\omega\big)_+dy$.
Every class with $v^\star_c>0$ is covered at exactly $1-\alpha$ by the (possibly randomised) optimum.
\end{proposition}

Three remarks guide the design.
(a) The weights are constants, one per class in each bin.
This per-class marginal programme is the analogue of MDCP's $x$-conditional one; MDCP's appendix states the same form with abstract density ratios \citep[Thm.~7]{yang2026multi}.
Under $Q_b$, Bayes' rule gives these ratios as $p_c(x)/\pi_{c,b}$, so a class posterior supplies them.
In classification, the analogous multi-constraint Neyman--Pearson sets are the class-specific-coverage sets of \citet{sadinle2019least}.
(b) With $v$ constant across classes the band is a level set of $\omega^{-1}$ times the posterior predictive mixture, which is the shortest marginally valid band in weighted length \citep{lei2013distribution,lei2014distribution}.
A weight $v_c$ above the common value trades length for class $c$'s coverage.
(c) In the population, the union or the maximum over class-wise valid bands is feasible for the same programme, so neither has a smaller weighted length than $C^\star$.
We use $\omega=1/\bar\sigma$, with $\bar\sigma(x)=\sum_c p_c(x)\sigma_c(x)$ the posterior-mean forecast scale.
With $K=1$ and a symmetric unimodal residual law, the band is then the normalised-residual band of FLARE \citep{lei2018distribution}.

\begin{remark}[why weighted length]
\label{rem:abs}
With $\omega\equiv1$ the optimum covers a point only where its density exceeds a level that is common to the whole bin.
Inside a bin, coverage is then spent on objects with sharp forecasts, and objects with uncertain forecasts are given up.
Scale normalisation optimises the weighted length, not the absolute length that we report; without it the bands are 2--16\% shorter in absolute length but trade coverage across objects and horizons (\cref{app:ablation}). The imbalance is small on our benchmarks, so the choice is a design preference for even coverage rather than an efficiency gain.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The three components}

\paragraph{C1: hypothesis-conditional forecaster.}
We train three additional LightGBM models, the median and the 16\%/84\% quantiles, with FLARE's recipe and the one-hot true class appended to the prefix features.
At prediction time we query them under every hypothesis $c$, obtaining $\hat\mu_c(x)$ and $\hat\sigma_c(x)=\max\{(\hat q_{84,c}-\hat q_{16,c})/2,\sigma_{\min}\}$.
All classes share trees, so a class with 100 objects borrows the dependence on cadence, colour and lead time from classes with thousands.
On the calibration objects (out-of-fold) we estimate, per class and bin, the density $\hat g_{c,b}$ of the standardised residual $(y-\hat\mu_c)/\hat\sigma_c$.
We use a Gaussian-smoothed histogram on $[-60,60]$ standardised units, wide enough for heavy-tailed residuals. Its bandwidth is twice Silverman's rule (chosen on validation folds, \cref{app:hyper}), and we pool the bins of a class when a bin has fewer than 150 points.
This gives $\hat f_c(y\mid x)=\hat g_{c,b}\big((y-\hat\mu_c(x))/\hat\sigma_c(x)\big)/\hat\sigma_c(x)$.
When a calibration point's density under its own class is evaluated, its own object's points are left out of $\hat g$.
This removes the main source of in-sample optimism, since a point's own kernel inflates its density.
The bandwidth and the pooling decision still use all objects.

\paragraph{C2: calibrated membership.}
\Cref{eq:oracle} uses $p_c$ as a likelihood ratio, not only through its argmax, and early classifiers of rare objects are poorly calibrated.
We therefore temperature-scale the classifier \citep{guo2017calibration}: $\hat p_c(x)\propto \tilde p_c(x_0)^{1/T}$, where $T$ minimises the negative log-likelihood of the calibration objects' true classes.
The classifier estimates $\P(Z=c\mid x_0)$, not $p_c(x)$ under $Q_b$, which also conditions on the lead time and on a point being observed.
If the two differ by class-level factors, $Q_b(Z=c\mid x)\propto\P(Z=c\mid x_0)\,a_{c,b}$, then the per-bin dual weights absorb the $a_{c,b}$, up to an input-dependent normalisation that changes the effective length weight.
Without this assumption \gamut{} only approximates the oracle.
Either way the posterior's error affects the width of the band, not the validity of the strict-split GAMUT protocol (\cref{thm:valid}).

\paragraph{C3: dual-weighted level-set calibration.}
For weights $v$ let $s_v(x,y)=\bar\sigma(x)\sum_c v_{c}\hat p_c(x)\hat f_c(y\mid x)$.
In bin $b$, let $L_i(t)$ be the fraction of object $i$'s bin-$b$ points that $\{y:s_v\ge t\}$ misses.
The per-class conformal risk-control threshold \citep{angelopoulos2024conformal} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:crc}
t_{c}(v)=\sup\Big\{t:\ \textstyle\sum_{i:z_i=c}L_i(t)\le \alpha(n_{c}+1)-1\Big\},
\end{equation}
where the sum and $n_c$ run over the class-$c$ objects with points in cell $b$.
The cells are FLARE's lead-time bins, merged with their neighbours until every class with at least $\lceil2/\alpha\rceil$ calibration objects overall has that many objects in each cell.
With four calibration folds this keeps 6 to 10 of ZTF's 10 bins at 68\% and 2 to 7 at 95\%, and all 6 bins on the UCR benchmarks. On PLAsTiCC, whose smallest classes have 30 to 40 calibration objects, it keeps 4 to 9 cells at 68\% but only 1 or 2 at 95\%. There, validity at 95\% is per class over all horizons, not per horizon (\cref{app:cert} lists every setting).
This is the analogue of FLARE's fallback for small Mondrian cells.
The merging uses calibration labels and lead times only, never outcomes.
The common threshold is $\hat t=\min_c t_c(v)$, taken over the classes with $n_c\ge 1/\alpha-1$.
For smaller classes only $t=-\infty$ (the whole line) would be valid, so we exclude them from the minimum.
The band is short only if no class's threshold lies far below the others.
At the oracle, every class with a positive multiplier is covered at exactly $1-\alpha$, which here means that its threshold equals the common one.
We search for such $v$ in each cell with a damped multiplicative fixed point:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dual}
v_c\leftarrow v_c\cdot\mathrm{clip}\big((\bar t/t_c(v))^{1/2},\tfrac12,2\big),
\end{equation}
where $\bar t$ is the geometric mean of the included $t_c(v)$, after which $v$ is renormalised to geometric mean one.
A class whose threshold is low is under-covered at the common level and gains weight.
The iteration starts from $v_c=1/\hat\pi_c$ (the class-balanced score) and stops when $\max_c|\log(t_c/\bar t)|<0.005$ or after 100 iterations.
The fixed point is a heuristic for the dual of \cref{prop:oracle}.
We do not prove that it converges.
A class whose constraint is slack keeps losing weight until the cap, which mimics a zero multiplier.
In our runs the median number of iterations per bin was \DualItersMedian{}, and \DualItersCapPct{} of the bins reached the cap (\DualItersCapSmall{} on PLAsTiCC-train and ECG5000 at 95\%).
The band $\{y:s_{\hat v}(x,y)\ge\hat t\}$ can be a union of intervals when the class forecasts disagree.
Coverage is computed from the exact score.
We report the length of the band on a grid of 400 uniform points over $\pm8\hat\sigma_c$ around the hypotheses with $\hat p_c\ge10^{-3}$, plus 49 points within $\pm3\hat\sigma_c$ of every hypothesis. Kandinsky CP and MDCP use the same grid.
Bins without calibration points borrow the nearest bin's $(v,\hat t)$.
\Cref{alg:gamut} summarises the procedure.
Calibration and prediction take \RuntimeGamutZTF{} seconds per fold on ZTF (\cref{app:runtime}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\gamut{} calibration and prediction}
\label{alg:gamut}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE calibration objects with true classes; hypothesis forecasts $\hat\mu_c,\hat\sigma_c$ (C1); probabilities $\tilde p$; level $\alpha$
\STATE $T\gets\argmin_T$ NLL of the true classes under $\tilde p^{1/T}$; $\hat p\gets\tilde p^{1/T}$ normalised \hfill (C2)
\STATE fit residual laws $\hat g_{c,b}$; score calibration points leave-one-object-out \hfill (C1)
\STATE merge lead-time bins into cells until every class with $\ge\lceil2/\alpha\rceil$ objects has that many per cell
\FOR{each horizon cell $b$}
\STATE $v\gets 1/\hat\pi$; iterate \cref{eq:dual} until the thresholds agree or 100 steps \hfill (C3)
\STATE $\hat t_b\gets\min_c t_c(v)$ over classes with $n_{c,b}\ge1/\alpha-1$; store $v_b$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{predict:} $C(x)=\{y:\bar\sigma(x)\sum_c v_{b(x),c}\,\hat p_c(x)\hat f_c(y\mid x)\ge \hat t_{b(x)}\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Guarantees}
\label{sec:theory}

\begin{theorem}[finite-sample class validity under the strict GAMUT protocol]
\label{thm:valid}
Assume the classifier and forecasters are trained on objects disjoint from the calibration and test objects.
Assume also that, within each class, the calibration objects and the test object are exchangeable.
Split the calibration objects, stratified by class, into halves $A$ and $B$.
On $A$ fit, by any rule, everything except the thresholds: the temperature, $\hat\pi$, the residual laws (bandwidth and pooling included), the horizon cells and the weights $v_b$.
On $B$ compute the thresholds of \cref{eq:crc}.
Let $n^B_{c,b}$ be the number of class-$c$ objects of $B$ with points in cell $b$.
Then, conditionally on $A$ and on the classes and cell memberships of the objects in $B$, every class $c$ with $n^B_{c,b}\ge1/\alpha-1$ satisfies $\cov_{c,b}\ge1-\alpha$ in every cell $b$ that has calibration points in $B$.
\end{theorem}

The proof combines per-class conformal risk control with the monotonicity of the band in $t$ (\cref{app:proofs}).
The guarantee needs only correct calibration labels (and the size condition).
A miscalibrated posterior or a misspecified $\hat f_c$ leaves it intact and changes only the width.
Kandinsky's fractional-membership guarantee, by contrast, needs the true $\P(Z\mid X,Y)$.
The default \gamut{} uses all calibration objects for both steps.
It approximates the oracle band and carries no finite-sample guarantee.
A uniform-convergence bound is given in \cref{app:proofs}, but its constants are too loose to be informative at our class sizes.
We therefore check validity empirically.
Every method in the main experiments uses cross-fitted base models (FLARE's protocol), whose training data include the test fold.
This places them outside \cref{thm:valid} \citep{barber2021predictive}.
A strict three-way split on ZTF, with disjoint training, calibration and test objects, is reported in \cref{app:cert}. In the tables, we call this strict-split evaluation ``GAMUT (strict split)''; it is the same GAMUT method under a different fitting protocol.
