\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Calibrating forecast bands by predicted types or classifier probabilities systematically under-covers rare, heterogeneous latent classes (\cref{prop:gap}), whereas worst-case class unions inflate widths across routine objects.
Derived from the shortest scale-weighted class-valid oracle band (\cref{prop:oracle}), \gamut{} combines hypothesis-conditional quantile forecasts (\textbf{C1}), temperature-scaled posteriors (\textbf{C2}), and threshold-equalising conformal risk control (\textbf{C3}).
Across ZTF-BTS, PLAsTiCC, UCR, and two untouched LSST WFD sets, cross-conformal \gamut{} restores rare-class validity while cutting widths by \WidthGainValidSixtyeight{} (68\%) and \WidthGainValidNinetyfive{} (95\%) against always-valid baselines, and strict-split \gamut{} (\cref{thm:valid}) certifies finite-sample coverage for every sufficiently populated class--horizon cell.
Learned empirical next-point laws with Venn--Abers recalibration likewise sharpen follow-up set-closure probabilities, motivating future extensions to hybrid rare-class fallbacks and Rubin Observatory alert streams.
