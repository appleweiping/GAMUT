This work develops conformal calibration methods designed to maintain reliable forecast uncertainty across every latent subpopulation---including rare classes---when group membership must be inferred from partial observations at test time.
In time-domain astronomy, calibrated rare-class forecast bands and next-observation closure probabilities support more equitable and efficient allocation of scarce spectroscopic telescope time to scientifically valuable transients such as superluminous supernovae and tidal disruption events.
More broadly, in domains where historical records carry ground-truth subgroup annotations that are unavailable at deployment (such as clinical disease subtypes), latent-class validity helps prevent minority subpopulations from being systematically under-covered behind seemingly well-calibrated aggregate metrics.
At the same time, per-class guarantees apply strictly to classes represented and accurately labelled in the calibration taxonomy, and formal finite-sample certification additionally requires strict data splitting and sufficient per-class calibration counts (\cref{app:limits}).
Subpopulations absent from the taxonomy or subject to systematic selection bias receive no automatic protection, and practitioners should audit taxonomy completeness and label provenance prior to operational deployment.
